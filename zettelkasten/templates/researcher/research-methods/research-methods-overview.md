# Research Methods Overview

Research methods are systematic approaches to gathering and analyzing information.

## Types of Research

### Quantitative Research
Numerical data and statistical analysis.

**Methods**:
- Surveys and questionnaires
- Experiments
- Statistical analysis
- Large sample sizes

**Strengths**:
- Objective measurements
- Statistical significance
- Generalizable results
- Replicable

**Example**: Survey 1000 users about app usage patterns, analyze with statistics.

### Qualitative Research
Non-numerical data, understanding meanings and experiences.

**Methods**:
- Interviews
- Focus groups
- Observations
- Case studies
- Content analysis

**Strengths**:
- Rich, detailed data
- Context and nuance
- Explores "why" and "how"
- Flexible

**Example**: Interview 20 users in-depth about their experiences with the app.

### Mixed Methods
Combines quantitative and qualitative approaches.

**Benefits**:
- Comprehensive understanding
- Triangulation of findings
- Strengths of both approaches

## Research Process

### 1. Define Research Question
Clear, focused question that guides the study.

**Good Questions**:
- ✅ "How does social media use affect teen mental health?"
- ✅ "What factors influence customer retention in SaaS?"

**Bad Questions**:
- ❌ "Is social media bad?" (Too vague)
- ❌ "Everything about customers" (Too broad)

### 2. Literature Review
Survey existing research on the topic.

**Purpose**:
- Understand current knowledge
- Identify gaps
- Avoid duplicating work
- Build theoretical framework

### 3. Design Study
Choose methods and plan data collection.

**Considerations**:
- Research question fit
- Available resources
- Ethical implications
- Feasibility

### 4. Collect Data
Gather information systematically.

**Best Practices**:
- Consistent procedures
- Detailed documentation
- Ethical treatment of participants
- Quality control

### 5. Analyze Data
Find patterns and draw conclusions.

**Quantitative**: Statistical tests, correlations, regressions
**Qualitative**: Coding, themes, patterns

### 6. Report Findings
Communicate results clearly.

**Structure**:
- Introduction and background
- Methods
- Results
- Discussion
- Conclusions

## Research Design Types

### Experimental
Manipulate variables to test causation.

**Example**: Test if new teaching method improves learning by randomly assigning students to control/treatment groups.

### Observational
Observe without intervention.

**Example**: Study how people naturally use a public space.

### Correlational
Examine relationships between variables.

**Example**: Analyze relationship between study hours and exam scores.

### Case Study
In-depth analysis of specific instance.

**Example**: Detailed study of one company's digital transformation.

## Sampling

### Probability Sampling
Every member has known chance of selection.

- **Random**: Everyone has equal chance
- **Stratified**: Sample from each subgroup
- **Cluster**: Sample groups, then individuals

### Non-Probability Sampling
Selection based on non-random criteria.

- **Convenience**: Easily accessible participants
- **Purposive**: Specific characteristics
- **Snowball**: Participants recruit others

## Validity and Reliability

### Validity
Does the study measure what it claims to measure?

- **Internal**: Are results due to what you think?
- **External**: Can results generalize?
- **Construct**: Does measure capture the concept?

### Reliability
Would the study produce consistent results if repeated?

- **Test-retest**: Same results over time?
- **Inter-rater**: Different observers agree?
- **Internal consistency**: Items measure same thing?

## Ethics in Research

### Key Principles
1. **Informed Consent**: Participants understand and agree
2. **Confidentiality**: Protect participant privacy
3. **No Harm**: Minimize risks to participants
4. **Honesty**: Report findings accurately
5. **Respect**: Value participants' autonomy

### IRB (Institutional Review Board)
Reviews research involving human subjects for ethical compliance.

## Common Pitfalls

❌ **Confirmation Bias**: Seeking data that supports hypothesis
❌ **Small Sample Size**: Insufficient data for conclusions
❌ **Confounding Variables**: Uncontrolled factors affect results
❌ **P-Hacking**: Manipulating data to get significant results
❌ **Cherry-Picking**: Reporting only favorable results
