# Critical Thinking

Critical thinking is the objective analysis and evaluation of issues to form judgment.

## Core Skills

### 1. Analysis
Break down complex information into components.

**Questions to Ask**:
- What are the key elements?
- How do parts relate to the whole?
- What patterns emerge?
- What's missing?

### 2. Evaluation
Assess credibility and quality of information.

**Criteria**:
- **Source**: Who created this? What's their expertise?
- **Evidence**: What supports the claims?
- **Logic**: Do conclusions follow from premises?
- **Bias**: What perspectives are missing?

### 3. Inference
Draw reasonable conclusions from evidence.

**Process**:
1. Gather relevant information
2. Identify patterns and connections
3. Consider alternative explanations
4. Form tentative conclusions
5. Test against new evidence

### 4. Synthesis
Combine information from multiple sources into coherent understanding.

**Techniques**:
- Compare and contrast sources
- Identify common themes
- Resolve contradictions
- Build integrated framework

## Logical Reasoning

### Deductive Reasoning
From general to specific.

```
All humans are mortal (general premise)
Socrates is human (specific case)
Therefore, Socrates is mortal (conclusion)
```

### Inductive Reasoning
From specific to general.

```
Swan 1 is white (observation)
Swan 2 is white (observation)
Swan 3 is white (observation)
Therefore, all swans are white (generalization)
# Note: This can be wrong! (Black swans exist)
```

### Abductive Reasoning
Best explanation for observations.

```
The grass is wet (observation)
It probably rained (best explanation)
# Could also be sprinklers, dew, etc.
```

## Common Logical Fallacies

### Ad Hominem
Attacking the person instead of the argument.

❌ "You can't trust his research because he's young."

### Straw Man
Misrepresenting opponent's argument to make it easier to attack.

❌ "She wants to reduce military spending, so she doesn't care about national security."

### False Dichotomy
Presenting only two options when more exist.

❌ "Either you support this policy completely, or you're against progress."

### Appeal to Authority
Accepting claim because authority said it.

❌ "Famous scientist says X, so X must be true."
✅ "Famous scientist says X, and here's their evidence..."

### Slippery Slope
Assuming one thing will inevitably lead to extreme outcome.

❌ "If we allow this small change, soon everything will collapse!"

### Hasty Generalization
Drawing broad conclusion from limited evidence.

❌ "I met two rude people from that city, so everyone there is rude."

## Evaluating Arguments

### Argument Structure
```
Premise 1: All mammals are warm-blooded
Premise 2: Whales are mammals
Conclusion: Therefore, whales are warm-blooded
```

### Questions to Ask
1. **Are premises true?** Check facts and evidence
2. **Is logic valid?** Does conclusion follow from premises?
3. **Are there hidden assumptions?** What's taken for granted?
4. **What's the counterargument?** Consider opposing views

## Information Evaluation

### CRAAP Test
- **Currency**: How recent is the information?
- **Relevance**: Does it relate to your question?
- **Authority**: Who is the author? What's their expertise?
- **Accuracy**: Is it supported by evidence?
- **Purpose**: Why was it created? What's the bias?

### Source Hierarchy (Generally)
1. **Peer-reviewed research**: Highest quality
2. **Expert analysis**: Subject matter experts
3. **Reputable news**: Fact-checked journalism
4. **General websites**: Variable quality
5. **Social media**: Lowest reliability

## Cognitive Biases

### Confirmation Bias
Seeking information that confirms existing beliefs.

**Counter**: Actively seek disconfirming evidence.

### Availability Heuristic
Overestimating likelihood of memorable events.

**Counter**: Look at actual statistics, not vivid examples.

### Anchoring Bias
Over-relying on first piece of information.

**Counter**: Consider multiple reference points.

### Dunning-Kruger Effect
Overconfidence from limited knowledge.

**Counter**: Recognize limits of your expertise.

## Developing Critical Thinking

### Practices
1. **Question Assumptions**: What are we taking for granted?
2. **Consider Alternatives**: What other explanations exist?
3. **Seek Evidence**: What supports this claim?
4. **Think About Thinking**: Metacognition
5. **Embrace Uncertainty**: Comfortable with "I don't know"

### Daily Habits
- Read diverse perspectives
- Discuss ideas with others
- Write to clarify thinking
- Reflect on decisions
- Learn from mistakes

## Socratic Questioning

### Six Types
1. **Clarification**: What do you mean by...?
2. **Assumptions**: What are you assuming?
3. **Evidence**: What evidence supports this?
4. **Perspectives**: What's another viewpoint?
5. **Implications**: What follows from this?
6. **Questions**: Why is this question important?
