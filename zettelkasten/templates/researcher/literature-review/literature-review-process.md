# Literature Review Process

A literature review surveys existing research on a topic to establish context and identify gaps.

## Purpose

### Why Literature Reviews?
1. **Understand Current Knowledge**: What do we already know?
2. **Identify Gaps**: What questions remain unanswered?
3. **Avoid Duplication**: Don't repeat existing work
4. **Build Foundation**: Establish theoretical framework
5. **Justify Research**: Show why your study matters

## Types of Literature Reviews

### Narrative Review
Traditional overview of a topic.

**Characteristics**:
- Broad scope
- Less systematic
- Author's interpretation
- Common in introductions

### Systematic Review
Rigorous, replicable methodology.

**Characteristics**:
- Defined search strategy
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Quality assessment
- Synthesizes all relevant studies

### Meta-Analysis
Quantitative synthesis of study results.

**Characteristics**:
- Statistical combination of findings
- Larger sample sizes
- More powerful conclusions

## The Review Process

### 1. Define Scope
```
❌ Too Broad: "Everything about AI"
✅ Focused: "Impact of large language models on education (2020-2024)"
```

**Questions to Answer**:
- What exactly am I reviewing?
- What time period?
- What types of sources?
- What aspects am I focusing on?

### 2. Search for Sources

#### Search Strategies
```
# Keyword search
("machine learning" OR "deep learning") AND education

# Boolean operators
NOT review  # Exclude review articles
AND empirical  # Include only empirical studies
```

#### Databases
- **Google Scholar**: Broad coverage
- **PubMed**: Medical/health sciences
- **IEEE Xplore**: Engineering/computer science
- **JSTOR**: Humanities/social sciences
- **Web of Science**: Citation tracking

#### Search Tips
- Use multiple databases
- Try different keyword combinations
- Follow citations (forward and backward)
- Set alerts for new publications

### 3. Screen Sources

#### Inclusion Criteria
- Relevant to research question
- Published in peer-reviewed journal
- Uses appropriate methods
- Within time frame

#### Exclusion Criteria
- Not peer-reviewed
- Wrong topic or population
- Insufficient detail
- Duplicate data

### 4. Extract Information

#### What to Record
- Full citation
- Research question
- Methods used
- Key findings
- Limitations
- Your notes/thoughts

#### Organization
```markdown
# Smith et al. (2023) - ML in Education

## RQ
How do students interact with LLM-based tutoring?

## Methods
- 100 college students
- Mixed methods (surveys + interviews)
- 6-week intervention

## Findings
- 80% found LLM helpful
- Concerns about over-reliance
- Need for guidance

## Limitations
- Small sample
- Single institution
- Self-reported data

## Notes
Connects to [[Student Learning Strategies]]
Contradicts [[Johnson 2022]] on over-reliance
```

### 5. Analyze and Synthesize

#### Find Patterns
- What do studies agree on?
- Where do they disagree?
- What methods are common?
- What gaps exist?

#### Organize Themes
```
# Theme 1: Benefits of LLMs in Education
- Smith (2023): Improved engagement
- Jones (2024): Better outcomes
- Lee (2024): Accessibility

# Theme 2: Concerns
- Brown (2023): Over-reliance
- Davis (2024): Critical thinking

# Theme 3: Best Practices
- Wilson (2023): Scaffolding
- Miller (2024): Metacognitive prompts
```

### 6. Write Review

#### Structure
1. **Introduction**: Topic, scope, why it matters
2. **Methods**: How you searched and selected
3. **Results/Themes**: Organized synthesis
4. **Discussion**: Patterns, gaps, implications
5. **Conclusion**: Summary and future directions

## Managing Sources

### Reference Managers
- **Zotero**: Free, open-source, excellent
- **Mendeley**: Free, social features
- **EndNote**: Commercial, powerful
- **Paperpile**: Web-based, Google Docs integration

### Organizing Notes
- Tag by theme
- Link related sources
- Note key quotes
- Track methodology
- Record your thoughts

## Critical Appraisal

### Evaluating Quality

#### Study Design
- Is method appropriate for question?
- Is sample size adequate?
- Are there confounding variables?

#### Analysis
- Are statistics correct?
- Are conclusions supported?
- Are limitations acknowledged?

#### Bias
- Funding sources?
- Conflicts of interest?
- Selective reporting?

### Quality Checklists
- PRISMA (Systematic Reviews)
- CASP (Critical Appraisal)
- JBI (Evidence Synthesis)

## Common Challenges

### Information Overload
**Solution**: Clear inclusion criteria, focus on high-quality sources

### Contradictory Findings
**Solution**: Discuss differences in methods, samples, contexts

### Publication Bias
**Solution**: Search for unpublished studies, grey literature

### Evolving Field
**Solution**: Set date range, note when search conducted

## Best Practices

1. **Be Systematic**: Document search strategy
2. **Be Inclusive**: Don't cherry-pick supporting evidence
3. **Be Critical**: Evaluate source quality
4. **Be Organized**: Use reference manager
5. **Be Current**: Include recent publications
6. **Be Transparent**: Report how many sources found/included

## Writing Tips

### Synthesize, Don't Just Summarize
```
❌ Summary: "Smith found X. Jones found Y. Lee found Z."
✅ Synthesis: "Three studies examining LLMs in education (Jones, 2024; Lee, 2024; Smith, 2023) consistently reported improved engagement, though effects varied by student prior knowledge."
```

### Compare and Contrast
```
While early studies emphasized benefits (Smith, 2020; Jones, 2021), recent research highlights potential drawbacks (Brown, 2023; Davis, 2024), suggesting a more nuanced understanding has emerged.
```

### Identify Gaps
```
Despite extensive research on LLMs for language learning, few studies have examined their effectiveness for STEM education, particularly in mathematics (notable exception: Wilson, 2024).
```
