## The "Learn to Code" Trap

### Don't Fall For It (Seriously)

**The conventional wisdom** (2020-2023):
> "Creative jobs disappearing? Learn to code! Programming is the future!"

**The 2025 reality**:
> "Programming jobs are ALSO disappearing. You'd be jumping from one sinking ship to another."

**Why coding isn't the answer**:

**1. AI codes better than junior developers already**
- [capability] GitHub Copilot writes 40-60% of code at Microsoft
- [capability] Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT can build entire features
- [trend] "Junior developer" role being hollowed out
- [timeline] Entry-level programming jobs down 30%+ by end of 2025

**2. The skill-acquisition race you can't win**
- [timeline] Learn to code: 12-24 months (bootcamp or self-taught)
- [timeline] AI improves: Every 3-6 months (exponential)
- [math] By the time you're job-ready, AI is 4-8 generations better
- [conclusion] You're always chasing a moving target that moves faster than you

**3. Saturation + automation = doom**
- [supply] Bootcamp grads + laid-off creatives + offshore devs = massive supply
- [demand] AI reduces need for human developers
- [result] Supply ↑, Demand ↓ = wages ↓, jobs ↓
- [data] Average bootcamp grad job search: 3-6 months in 2024, 9-12 months in 2025

**4. Mid-level is next**
- [current] AI writes components/features
- [2026-2027] AI writes entire systems
- [2028+] AI does architecture? (we'll see)
- [implication] Mid-level devs are just "slower, more expensive AI"

**Better options than coding**:
- Physical trades (electrician, plumber) - **10-year horizon**
- Healthcare (nursing, PT) - **15-year horizon**
- AI-augmented creative director - **5-7 year horizon**
- Literally anything involving physical presence - **varies**

**Exception**: If you LOVE coding for its own sake, go ahead. But don't do it just for job security - that ship sailed.

## What's Actually Defensible?

### Jobs with Moats (For Now)

**1. Physical World Interaction**
- Can't automate through software alone
- Requires robots (decades behind)
- Examples: Trades, nursing, physical therapy

**2. Human Trust/Connection**
- Humans prefer humans for high-stakes/emotional work
- Examples: Therapy, high-end sales, executive coaching
- Caveat: This is cultural, not technical (could change)

**3. Creative Vision (Taste)**
- AI generates, human curates
- Requires judgment, cultural context
- Examples: Creative director, brand strategist, museum curator
- Caveat: Shrinking role (fewer humans needed)

**4. Complex Coordination**
- Multi-stakeholder, political, contextual
- Examples: Program management, diplomacy, C-suite
- Caveat: Smaller teams (AI handles tactical, human handles strategic)

**5. Regulatory Capture**
- Jobs protected by law/regulation
- Examples: Licensed therapists, doctors (diagnosis), lawyers (in some jurisdictions)
- Caveat: Regulations can change (lobbying by AI companies)
