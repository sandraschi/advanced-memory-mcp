# Show Don't Tell

The fundamental principle of engaging fiction: demonstrate experiences through sensory details and actions rather than stating facts.

## The Difference

### Telling
```
John was angry.
```

Simple statement. Reader is told how to feel.

### Showing
```
John's fists clenched. His jaw tightened. He stared at the wall,
breathing hard through his nose.
```

Sensory details. Reader experiences and deduces anger.

## Why Show?

### Engagement
Showing engages imagination. Reader participates in constructing meaning.

### Emotional Impact
Felt emotions are stronger than described emotions.

### Trust
Showing respects reader intelligence. Allows personal interpretation.

## When to Show

### Important Moments
- Emotional turning points
- Character revelations
- Climactic scenes
- First impressions

### Examples
```
❌ Tell: "She was terrified of the dark basement."
✅ Show: "Sarah's hand trembled on the light switch. The basement
stairs descended into blackness. She swallowed hard, her heart
pounding in her ears."
```

## When to Tell

### Transitions
```
Three weeks passed. John trained every day.
```

Efficient. Not every moment needs full detail.

### Pacing
Use telling to speed through less important periods.

### Background Information
```
The town had been founded in 1842 by Welsh miners.
```

Historical facts don't need sensory detail.

## Techniques for Showing

### Use Sensory Details
Engage all five senses.

```
The coffee shop smelled of cinnamon and burnt espresso. Steam
fogged the windows. Jazz played softly, nearly drowned by the
hiss of the milk frother.
```

### Physical Reactions
Show emotion through body language.

**Fear**:
- Racing heart
- Shallow breathing
- Cold sweat
- Frozen muscles

**Anger**:
- Flushed face
- Clenched fists
- Tight jaw
- Raised voice

**Love**:
- Warm chest
- Soft smile
- Gentle touch
- Lingering gaze

### Action and Behavior
Character actions reveal personality.

```
❌ Tell: "Mark was generous."
✅ Show: Mark always ordered extra coffee for the security guard
working the night shift.
```

### Dialogue
Let characters reveal themselves.

```
"I'm fine," she said, not looking up from her phone.

vs.

"I'm absolutely wonderful, can't you tell?" she snapped, throwing
her phone across the room.
```

## The Balance

### Best Prose Uses Both
- Show for important moments
- Tell for transitions and pacing
- Vary rhythm for engagement

### Example
```
The argument had lasted three hours (telling - efficient transition).
Now they sat in silence. Sarah stared at the ring on her finger,
turning it slowly. Michael watched her, his coffee growing cold.
Neither spoke, but the weight of unspoken words filled the room
(showing - important emotional moment).
```

## Common Pitfalls

### Over-Showing
```
❌ Sarah walked to the door. She reached for the handle. She grasped
it with her fingers. She turned the handle clockwise. She pulled
the door toward her body. She stepped through the doorway.
```

**Too much detail slows pacing to a crawl.**

✅ Better: "Sarah left the room."

### Purple Prose
```
❌ The resplendent sun cascaded its golden tendrils across the
azure canvas of the heavens, each ray a symphony of luminous
beauty dancing upon the emerald tapestry below.
```

**Over-written. Distracts from story.**

✅ Better: "Sunlight streamed across the grass."

### Filtering
Unnecessary distance words.

```
❌ I saw the door open. I heard footsteps. I felt afraid.
```

In first-person POV, these words are implied.

✅ Better: "The door opened. Footsteps. My heart raced."
