## 📚 Philosophy of Mind (702 lines)

### Mind-Body Problem
**Four major positions**:

1. **Dualism** (Descartes)
   - Mind and body separate substances
   - Problems: Interaction, evolution, parsimony
   - "Cogito, ergo sum"

2. **Physicalism**
   - Identity Theory: Mental states = Brain states
   - Functionalism: Mental states = Functional roles
   - Eliminative Materialism: Mental states don't exist

3. **Property Dualism**
   - Emergentism: Consciousness emerges from complexity
   - Panpsychism: Consciousness fundamental (everything conscious to some degree)

4. **Idealism**
   - Only minds and ideas exist (not covered in detail)

### The Hard Problem (David Chalmers)

**Easy Problems**: Mechanism questions (solvable by neuroscience)
- How does brain process information?
- How does brain control behavior?
- How do we focus attention?

**Hard Problem**: Why is there subjective experience?
- Why doesn't processing happen "in the dark"?
- What is it LIKE to see red?
- Explanatory gap: Physical → Experience
- Philosophical zombies: Conceivable beings without consciousness

### Theories of Consciousness

1. **Global Workspace Theory** (Bernard Baars)
   - Consciousness as broadcast mechanism
   - Information in spotlight becomes conscious

2. **Integrated Information Theory** (Giulio Tononi)
   - Consciousness = Integrated information (Φ)
   - Quantifiable measure
   - Leads toward panpsychism

3. **Higher-Order Thought Theory**
   - Consciousness requires thought about thought
   - Metacognition creates awareness

4. **Attention Schema Theory** (Michael Graziano)
   - Consciousness as brain's model of attention
   - Useful illusion for control

### Qualia (Subjective Experience)

**Famous Thought Experiments**:

1. **Mary's Room** (Frank Jackson)
   - Neuroscientist knows all physical facts about color
   - Never seen color (black-white room)
   - Question: Does she learn something when she sees red?
   - Implication: Physical facts ≠ All facts?

2. **What Is It Like to Be a Bat?** (Thomas Nagel)
   - Bat echolocation - alien subjective experience
   - Can't know what it's like
   - Consciousness essentially involves point of view
   - Objective science can't capture subjective

3. **Inverted Spectrum**
   - Could your red be my green?
   - Qualia are private, incommunicable

### The Binding Problem

- Color, motion, location processed separately
- How unified experience?
- No single "place" where it comes together
- Proposals: Synchronized firing, re-entrant processing

### Free Will

**Three Positions**:

1. **Libertarian**: Free will exists, determinism false
2. **Hard Determinism**: Determinism true, no free will
3. **Compatibilism**: Free will compatible with determinism (most popular)

**Libet Experiments**:
- Brain activity precedes conscious decision (~300ms)
- Interpretation contested
- Doesn't decisively refute free will

### Personal Identity

**What makes you the same person over time?**

1. **Psychological Continuity**: Memories, personality
2. **Biological Continuity**: Same body/brain
3. **Bundle Theory** (Hume): No self, just experiences

**Thought Experiments**:
- Teleporter: Destroys and recreates you
- Brain hemisphere transplant: Which is you?
- Fission problem

### Intentionality

- How can thoughts be ABOUT things?
- Physical brain states → Aboutness?
- Theories: Causal, teleosemantic, interpretive

### AI Consciousness

**Can Machines Be Conscious?**

**Arguments For**:
- Functionalism: Right functions = Consciousness
- IIT: High Φ = Consciousness
- Computationalism: Mind is software

**Arguments Against**:
- Chinese Room (Searle): Symbol manipulation ≠ Understanding
- Biological naturalism: Requires biological substrate
- Quantum mind (Penrose): Needs quantum effects

**Current Status**: Don't know if LLMs conscious (probably not, but ?)

## 📖 Theory of Knowledge (372 lines)

### Classical Definition

**Knowledge as Justified True Belief (JTB)**:

1. **Belief**: You must believe P
2. **Truth**: P must be true
3. **Justification**: You must have good reasons

### Gettier Problem (1963)

**Bombshell**: JTB is NOT sufficient for knowledge!

**Famous Cases**:
- Job case: Right conclusion from false premise
- Barn facades: True by environmental luck

**Impact**: Destroyed 2000-year consensus, still unsolved

### Theories of Justification

1. **Foundationalism**
   - Knowledge built on foundations
   - Basic beliefs (self-justifying)
   - Derived beliefs (inferred from basic)
   - Problem: Which foundations? Regress?

2. **Coherentism**
   - Knowledge as coherent web
   - Beliefs justify each other holistically
   - No privileged foundations
   - Problem: Isolation (coherent fairy tales?)

3. **Reliabilism**
   - Knowledge from reliable processes
   - Perception, memory, logic
   - Problem: What counts as "reliable enough"?

### Skepticism

**Can we know anything about external world?**

1. **Descartes' Method**:
   - Systematic doubt
   - Senses deceive
   - Dreaming argument
   - Evil demon
   - Modern: Brain in vat

2. **Responses**:
   - Moorean: "Here is a hand" (common sense)
   - Contextualism: Standards shift by context
   - Pragmatic: Must act on best evidence

### Rationalism vs Empiricism

**Rationalism** (Descartes, Leibniz):
- Knowledge from reason alone
- A priori knowledge (math, logic)
- Innate ideas

**Empiricism** (Locke, Hume):
- All knowledge from experience
- Blank slate (tabula rasa)
- No innate ideas

**Kant's Synthesis**:
- Synthetic a priori knowledge exists
- Mind structures experience
- Categories: Space, time, causation

### Problem of Induction (Hume)

**The Challenge**:
- Sun rose every day in past
- Therefore will rise tomorrow?
- How justify this inference?
- Can't use induction to justify induction (circular)

**Status**: Still unsolved!

**Responses**:
- Pragmatic: Best we have
- Probabilistic: Bayesian updating
- Falsificationism: Science uses deduction (Popper)

### Social Epistemology

**Testimony**: Most knowledge from others
- How justified?
- Trust and reliability
- Current crisis: Misinformation

**Epistemic Injustice** (Miranda Fricker):
- Testimonial: Credibility deficit
- Hermeneutical: Lack of concepts
- Connects epistemology to social justice
