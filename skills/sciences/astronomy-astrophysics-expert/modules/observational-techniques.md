# Observational Techniques & Instrumentation

**Confidence**: 🟢 HIGH
**Last updated**: 2025-11-09
**Primary references**: [Webb-ESA-2025], [ELT-Wiki-2025]

## Instrument landscape
- **Space-based**:
  - **JWST** (6.5 m, L2): NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, NIRISS enabling spectroscopy 0.6–28 µm.
  - **Euclid** (1.2 m): VIS optical imager (0.1″/pixel) + NISP near-IR spectrophotometer.
- **Ground-based**:
  - **ELT (39 m)** under construction with adaptive optics providing 0.005″ resolution; first light expected 2029.
  - VLT, ALMA, and other facilities continue to deliver optical/NIR/sub-mm coverage with interferometry and high-dispersion spectroscopy.

## Choosing an instrument
| Science goal | Recommended toolset |
| --- | --- |
| Exoplanet atmospheres | JWST (transit/emission), ground-based high-resolution spectrographs (CRIRES+, ESPRESSO), upcoming ARIEL. |
| High-redshift galaxies | JWST deep fields, Euclid wide survey for statistics, ELT (future) for kinematics. |
| Stellar populations | Gaia astrometry, HST/JWST imaging, ELT/HARMONI integral-field spectroscopy. |
| Solar system | JWST NIRSpec for thermal mapping, ALMA for sub-mm, JUICE/Europa Clipper in-situ instruments. |

## Techniques checklist
1. **Define wavelength regime** – choose instruments covering required spectral range.
2. **Resolution needs** – spatial (″), spectral (R), temporal cadence.
3. **Signal-to-noise** – consider magnitude/flux, exposure time calculators, background sources.
4. **Adaptive optics/interferometry** – mention for high-resolution ground-based work.
5. **Data reduction** – reference pipelines (JWST DMS, ESO Reflex, Euclid processing functions).

## Assistant tips
- Provide direct links to exposure time calculators or proposal tools when advising observers.
- Mention data accessibility (MAST for JWST, ESA SODA for Euclid, ESO Science Archive).
- Clarify proprietary periods and community data release policies.
- Encourage calibration strategy planning (standard stars, sky flats).

## Quick numbers
- JWST diffraction limit at 2 µm ≈ 0.07″; MIRI at 7.7 µm ≈ 0.25″.
- ELT collecting area ≈ 978 m² (~15× Hubble).
- ALMA longest baselines deliver ~0.01″ at 350 GHz.
